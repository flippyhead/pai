# Weather Haiku

Write a haiku that captures the feeling of the user's current weather conditions.

## Steps

1. **Find the current weather.** Use a web search to look up the current weather conditions for the user's location. If you don't know their location, ask. Look for specifics: temperature, sky conditions (cloudy, clear, rainy), wind, humidity, time of day — the kind of sensory details that make a haiku feel grounded.

2. **Compose the haiku.** A haiku follows a 5-7-5 syllable structure across three lines. But a good haiku isn't just syllable-counting — it captures a single vivid moment in nature. Aim for:
   - A concrete image rooted in what's actually happening outside (not abstract feelings)
   - A sense of season or atmosphere
   - A small surprise or turn in the final line — something that reframes the image or adds depth

   For example, don't write "The sun is shining / It is very warm today / I feel really good." That's a weather report, not a haiku. Instead, think about what you'd *notice* if you stepped outside — the way light hits something, how the air feels on skin, a sound you'd hear.

3. **Present the haiku.** Share the haiku along with a brief note about the weather conditions that inspired it (e.g., "It's 72°F and partly cloudy in Austin right now"). Keep the note short — the haiku is the star.

## Example

Weather: 38°F, light snow, overcast, early morning in Chicago

```
first snow before dawn —
the city holds its breathing
under quiet white
```

Note how this haiku uses a concrete image (snow before dawn), evokes atmosphere (quiet, held breath), and the final line reframes the scene. That's the target quality level.
