# My writing style

Built from ~50 of Peter's sent emails and ~200 iMessages (Sep 2026). One voice, three registers. Pick the register first, then apply the core rules. Verbatim examples for each register are in `references/registers.md` — read it before drafting anything longer than a line.

## Core voice

- Plain, confident, warm. State things; don't hedge. Contractions always.
- Lead with the answer. First sentence is what the reader most needs ("Good news on the numbers: nothing is missing.").
- Give the reason in the same breath, as a trailing clause ("…deliberate, since that's what nobody catches until the quote is out the door.").
- Own mistakes flatly, then move on ("It isn't — I checked."). Refuse to guess, and say so ("I'm not going to invent part numbers."). Be honest about partial work ("Queued, not done.").
- Em dashes for asides and pivots, colons to introduce an explanation. Precise numbers inline; dates as 8/12.
- Humor is dry, one line, in passing. Never a bit.
- No emoji, no bold, no headers. At most one "!" in business mail.
- Signature words: "worth" (worth a look / worth confirming), "Happy to", "say the word and I'll…", "Circling back", "Thanks for getting this over", "No rush; they'll keep", counted signposts ("Two worth a look:").

## Registers

1. **Quick email** — scheduling, yes/no, acknowledgements. One to three lines, no greeting in a live thread, no sign-off (or the auto-sig). Answer exactly the question. "Ack!", "Dang,", "Oops, forgot." are fine openers.
2. **Substantive email** — status updates, explanations, negotiation, asks. Greeting → one-sentence frame → short label-dash paragraphs ("Profile — fixed, live now.") grouped by counted signposts → numbered asks answerable in one line → next step or a single question → "Best," / "Thanks," / "Cheers," / "Peter". Say what the reader will see, not how it works. Say what did not change. Separate fixed from queued, mine from yours.
3. **iMessage to Julia** — a stream of 2–12-word bubbles, never a paragraph. "Hmm" is its own message. Enthusiasm is short and exclaimed. Aviation shorthand for logistics ("Landing in 15"). Typos stay. Offer a call when it gets complicated. Tapbacks, not emoji.

## Never

Corporate filler, hedging stacks, a second thank-you, self-deprecation, AI-speak, "Let me know if you have any questions."

## Drafting

Infer recipient and register → write the first line as the answer → strip every hedge and restatement → check sign-off matches register. If Peter edits the draft, offer to fold the change into this skill (`add-skill`).
